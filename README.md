**Moving Away from Gas-Fired Kilns**

Historically, the ceramic industry has relied heavily on gas-fired kilns for firing products. While effective, these kilns contribute significantly to carbon emissions and environmental degradation. Recognizing the need for sustainable practices, Canadian manufacturers are exploring alternative technologies to mitigate their carbon footprint.​

A notable example is Duravit's initiative to construct a climate-neutral ceramics plant in Canada. This facility will utilize the world's first electric roller kiln powered entirely by hydroelectricity, demonstrating a commitment to sustainable manufacturing. By adopting this innovative technology, Duravit aims to save approximately 11,000 tons of CO₂ annually compared to conventional ceramic factories, setting a precedent for the industry.

**Hydrogen-Powered Kilns: Reducing Emissions**

Hydrogen-powered kilns present another promising avenue for reducing emissions in ceramic manufacturing. By replacing natural gas with hydrogen, these kilns can significantly lower carbon emissions, as hydrogen combustion produces only water vapor. Internationally, the H2Frit project has advanced hydrogen usage in ceramic manufacturing, with equipment installations at the Esmalglass plant marking a significant milestone.
In the UK, a groundbreaking trial successfully fired a custom-designed ceramics pilot kiln using 100% hydrogen, providing crucial insights into the feasibility of hydrogen as a clean fuel source in ceramic production. These international developments offer valuable lessons for Canadian manufacturers considering hydrogen-powered kilns.

**Government Incentives for Cleaner Kiln Technologies**

The Canadian government is actively promoting the adoption of clean technologies through various financial incentives. The Clean Technology Investment Tax Credit (Clean Tech ITC) offers a refundable tax credit of up to 30% for investments in eligible clean technology properties. This initiative aims to support businesses transitioning to sustainable practices, including the adoption of electric and hydrogen kilns.​ Additionally, the Clean Technology Manufacturing Investment Tax Credit provides a 30% refundable tax credit for investments in new machinery and equipment used in clean technology manufacturing and processing. These incentives significantly reduce the financial barriers associated with upgrading to cleaner kiln technologies, encouraging manufacturers to invest in sustainable solutions.​ 

**Conclusion**

The shift towards electric and hydrogen kilns represents a transformative movement in Canada's ceramic industry, aligning with global sustainability goals and reducing environmental impact. Innovative projects like Duravit's climate-neutral plant exemplify the potential of electric kilns powered by renewable energy sources. Simultaneously, international advancements in hydrogen-powered kilns provide a roadmap for reducing emissions in ceramic manufacturing. With robust government incentives supporting the adoption of clean technologies, Canadian ceramic manufacturers are well-positioned to lead the industry towards a more sustainable and environmentally friendly future.
